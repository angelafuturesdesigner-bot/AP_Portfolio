01

My inspiration

Since childhood, exploring nature has been one of my greatest passions. Today, climate change and waste pollution are destroying our planet: the Earth is running out of time, and humanity has contaminated the environment on an unprecedented scale.

Most of my work draws on nature and the principles of biomimicry. This project was inspired by the aesthetic and the function of the cryosphere, after a journey to Iceland revealed to me the profound consequences of global warming.

It was the sight of the ice melting that set the direction of the project. From the outset, I sought to understand the causes of ice melting, and that search led me to waste pollution and to the damage humanity has inflicted on our oceans and landfills.

02

Design process

Within waste pollution, I went in search of the hardest case, and found it in MLPs (multi-layered plastics), such as toothpaste tubes. Because they contain a layer of aluminium, they are among the most difficult materials to biodegrade or recycle. Existing methods fall short: they either separate each material, an expensive process, or rely on ‘waste to energy’ techniques, which reduce waste only in part. A new strategy was needed.

I resolved to recycle them myself. I gathered toothpaste tubes and processed them in the university workshop, shredding them into flakes and pressing the flakes into sheets.

After three rounds of testing, a new material emerged, with a striking aesthetic. It could be shredded and pressed once more, and so it remained recyclable.

Its appearance held a further surprise. It takes on the white and blue of the ice, and its shiny, heat-reflective surface recalls the texture of a glacier. In its properties, it echoes the primary role of the cryosphere.

With the material in my hands, the question became what it should become, and where it could matter most. The answer led me to Chennai, South India. There, the intense heat drives an overproduction of MLPs; much of the population lives in deprived houses near the dumpsites; and for many years its inhabitants have earned a living by collecting and selling waste.

In homes like these, heat and humidity enter through the roof. This, together with the heat-reflective nature of the material, drew my attention to roofs, and to the idea of turning the material into roof tiles.

03

How does it work?

The tile performs the same function as the cryosphere, which acts as the Earth’s thermostat: the deep freeze regulates the planet’s temperature by reflecting the heat of the Sun. The tile does the same on a roof.

By reflecting heat back into the atmosphere, the tile lowers indoor temperature and humidity.

Before making the final tiles, I tested their profile and the way they overlap through paper and foam models. The shape ensures stability, safety, self-ventilation and ergonomics: the bent edges allow the roof to be fixed easily and safely, creating a water-resistant, heat-reflective roof designed specifically for deprived houses.

04

Why is it different?

Several existing projects collect recyclable plastics, offering a source of income to end poverty and protect the oceans from pollution. Their strategies focus on reusing recyclable waste, yet very few have chosen to collect and process non-recyclable waste such as MLPs, which in theory cannot be processed at all. Many of them, moreover, seek to grow their turnover by selling products abroad.

Wastly takes a different path. It proposes a business model that allows disadvantaged people to earn a living by making their own products locally. Since these communities already collect and sell waste, they can also process it and produce the tiles themselves, for their own homes. For this reason, I designed a new NGO, ‘Wastly’, dedicated to raising the wages and livelihoods of vulnerable communities.

Wastly aims to restore ecosystems by reducing non-recyclable waste, to bring lasting value to India’s poorest communities, and to regenerate local economies. Its strategy would increase annual wages by 100% while reducing MLP waste in landfills, giving Chennai’s residents the welfare and dignified housing they deserve.

The same approach can be applied elsewhere in the world, wherever similar climates, poverty, social inequality and waste management challenges meet.

Built on the principles of the circular economy and sustainability, this business model could improve the livelihoods of millions of people living in the most deprived areas of the world.

A new Service Platform - from on field research in China to the final UI

NDA · VISUALS & CONTENTS ANONYMISED
Skills developed
Field research in International Markets Data Analysis with AI Service Mapping & Systems Thinking End-to-end UI Ownership Complex Dataset Management (Airtable)
The problem

An industrial manufacturer offered a wide range of services around its machines, but they lived in separate places and were largely unknown to customers, even the client itself could not say which ones deserved investment.
The goal: one platform that makes every service discoverable and sellable alongside the machine.

Outcome

A clear understanding of their active digital services, thanks to the service journey we built — which became the client's own decision-making tool for what to keep, drop, or develop. The platform's visual concept was shaped around their brand identity, alongside the design of 20 new features across four sprints, with the UI signed off through consistently positive feedback.

02

A new Design System - from fragmented design to one coherent digital ecosystem

NDA · VISUALS & CONTENTS ANONYMISED
Skills developed
Design System Architecture Figma Variables Management Stakeholder Trust Building Independent Project Ownership Onboarding & Training Design-Dev Collaboration
The problem

Years of growth had left an industrial product with no coherent design system: duplicated components with no shared foundation, interpreted differently by every designer and developer. UX and UI felt fractured screen to screen, handoffs to development were slow and error-prone, and weak cross-team communication let the gaps widen further.

Outcome

I rebuilt the design system on shared variables and foundations, aligned with the core component library, requesting changes through a structured process when needed and documenting everything to keep design and development consistent. I also designed new features end to end across desktop, tablet and mobile, working closely with developers to keep design and build in sync, and aligning UX and UI with the client's other product into one coherent digital ecosystem.

Project Management - recovering a delayed development programme

NDA · VISUALS & CONTENTS ANONYMISED
Skills developed
Delivery recovery Airtable system design Claude automation Root-cause reporting
The problem

The product is a modular software platform for sheet metal fabrication, used by over 1M active users. Our team designed its UX/UI from the ground up, then took on project management of the development team (around 10 developers) building it. At the point I stepped into that role, delivery was running 35% behind schedule, with no structured visibility into the source or scale of the delay. We took on project management to restore delivery control and establish clear accountability for its causes.

Outcome

I designed a dedicated Airtable database, connected to Claude, that captures task updates, deadlines and workload automatically and calculates delay — by cause — for every task, developer and sprint. Purpose-built Airtable interfaces turn this into a live programme-health view for the client: overall delay, root causes and the mitigation proposed for each. Delay is now measured and explained systematically, not reported anecdotally.

Tracking and logging my weekly hours was becoming time-consuming, especially in weeks spent across several projects at once. I mapped out the manual process I was using, then built an artifact in Claude connected to my Google Calendar based on the pain point identified: I set my planned hours per project for the week, the artifact reads my calendar, and it automatically checks actual hours against plan.
This lets me monitor my workload on each project week by week — avoiding overwork on billable projects — and makes logging hours in Zoho fast and straightforward at the end of the week
